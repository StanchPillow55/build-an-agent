## Slide 1: Content Slide 1

```markdown
**Speaker Notes:**

Imagine the present tense as the heartbeat of a conversation in Spanish. Just like our heart beats constantly, present tense verbs describe actions occurring at this moment or regularly.

Today, we’ll explore the fundamentals of present tense verbs in Spanish. These verbs help express what we’re doing now or routinely, like "I eat" or "She works." Conjugating verbs is crucial because it allows us to accurately convey who is performing the action.

Consider verb conjugation as setting the stage for a play—each verb must be in the right form to fit its role in the sentence.

To ensure understanding, think about this: How would you describe your daily routine using present tense verbs? What verbs are key to your day-to-day activities?

Let's dive in and start mastering these essential building blocks of Spanish communication.
```

## Slide 2: Content Slide 2

```markdown
**Speaker Notes for Slide 2:**

*Hook:* Think of conjugating verbs like dressing them for different occasions. Each subject (I, you, we) needs its own outfit (ending).

**Key Explanation:**
- **-ar verbs (e.g., hablar):** Remove the -ar and add endings: -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. Example: yo hablo, tú hablas.
- **-er verbs (e.g., comer):** Remove the -er and add endings: -o, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en. Example: yo como, él come.
- **-ir verbs (e.g., vivir):** Remove the -ir and add endings: -o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en. Example: nosotros vivimos, ellos viven.

*Closing Question:* Can anyone give a sentence using the verb 'comer' in the third person plural? This will help transition to practicing more sentences.
```
